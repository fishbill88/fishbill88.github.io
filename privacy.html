Privacy policy

TaskTopFight has player accounts. Your account, your save and the online features (friends, mail, the player stall, leaderboards and co-op rooms) run on a server we operate. This page lists what that server stores, why, how long it is kept and how to have it deleted. There are no ads, no third-party analytics, and nothing is sold or shared for marketing.

Last updated 16 October 2026.

Who is responsible

TaskTopFight is made by fishbill88, who controls the data described here. Contact: tasktopfight@gmail.com.

What the game reads on your PC, and keeps there

The fight escalates the longer you leave the keyboard alone, so the game has to know whether you are at the desk. It reads two things locally:

  • Whether input is happening. The game watches for mouse and keyboard activity to measure how many seconds have passed since the last one. It records that input occurred, never what was typed or clicked. It is not a keylogger and it captures no keystroke content of any kind.
  • Whether sound is playing, and whether an app is full-screen. This is how a film holds the idle clock instead of resetting it. The game reads audio peak levels and the system's full-screen state. It does not read what is playing, which application is playing it, or any audio content.

Both checks run on your computer and produce nothing but a number of seconds. That number is never sent to us. You can turn the audio check off under Idle while watching in the ⚙ panel.

What our server stores

  • Your account. Username, email address, character name, and a salted hash of your password (never the password itself). If you sign in with Steam, we receive your Steam ID from Valve to recognise you; we never see your Steam password or payment details.
  • Your save. Your hero's progress (level, gear, skills, settings, trophies and statistics), plus the last 30 earlier versions so a bad sync can be undone. Each save is stored with your computer's name and a network adapter (MAC) address, which tell your PCs apart when you play on more than one, so the game can ask which save to keep.
  • Sign-in sessions. A hashed sign-in token per device, with when it was created and last used. Sessions expire after 90 days.
  • Online features. Your friends list and friend requests; your online status (when you were last seen and which game mode or room you are in), shared with your friends; letters in your mailbox; items you list, buy or sell on the player stall; and your scores on the leaderboards.
  • Crash reports. When the game hits an error, it sends the error message and where in the code it happened, with your account, your computer's name and a MAC address. We keep the newest reports only.
  • IP addresses. Our server sees the address you connect from. It is used to limit sign-up and sign-in abuse (for example, too many failed passwords from one address). Sign-up counts per address are deleted after about two days.

What other players can see

Your character name, class, level and scores appear on leaderboards, in co-op rooms, on nameplates and on the player stall. Your friends can also see when you are online and which mode you are playing. Your username, email address, computer name and MAC address are never shown to other players.

Why we use it

  • To run the game you asked for: keep your save, sign you in, and provide friends, mail, trading, leaderboards and co-op.
  • To keep the game working and fair: fix crashes, stop abuse, and keep cheated scores off the boards.
  • To send password-reset emails, and only those. We do not send marketing email.

Who else handles it

  • Our hosting provider runs the machine the server is on. It stores the data on our behalf and does not use it for itself.
  • An email delivery service sends password-reset emails, so it receives your email address and the reset message.
  • Valve, if you play through Steam: it handles your purchase, your Steam account and game updates under Valve's privacy policy, and confirms your Steam ID to us when you sign in.
  • GitHub, if you play the version downloaded from our website: the game checks GitHub for updates and downloads them from there, under GitHub's own privacy policy.

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with anyone else unless the law requires it.

How long it is kept

Your account, save and online data are kept for as long as your account exists. Sign-in sessions expire after 90 days, older save versions roll off after 30, crash reports are trimmed to the newest ones, and sign-up counts per IP address go after about two days. When you ask us to delete your account, everything listed above goes with it.

Your save on your PC

The game also keeps a copy of your save in its own data folder on your PC, so you can carry on when the server cannot be reached. Ctrl+Alt+R wipes that local copy and starts a fresh world.

Children

TaskTopFight is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly hold accounts for them. If you believe a child under 13 has an account, write to us and we will delete it.

Your rights

You can ask to see the data we hold about you, to have it corrected, to receive a copy of it, or to have your account and everything attached to it deleted. Write to tasktopfight@gmail.com from the email address on your account, or tell us your character name if you sign in with Steam. We answer within 30 days. You can also complain to your local data protection authority; in the Philippines that is the National Privacy Commission.

Security

Connections to our account server are encrypted (HTTPS). Co-op room traffic carries game state and character names only. Passwords and sign-in tokens are stored only as hashes. Repeated failed sign-ins lock the account for a short time.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts storing something new, this page will be updated before that version ships, and the change will be called out in the update notes. The date at the top always reflects the current text.
